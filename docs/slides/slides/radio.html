GETTING IT ON THE AIR

A script puts the objects near you on 144.39

ASKS

Every two minutes

It asks the feeder for the objects within a chosen distance of your station.

PICKS

The few that matter

Medium and up, ten objects at most, plus a bulletin for anything critical.

SENDS

Through your KISS TNC

Direwolf or a hardware TNC, under your own callsign, in third-party form.

python3 aprs_radio.py --server https://aprs-incidents.phlm.sh --callsign W3XYZ-10 \
--lat 40.12 --lon -75.34 --kiss-tcp 127.0.0.1:8001

It needs only Python and prints what it would send until you add --transmit. Add --auto to carry everything nearby, slowly, most important first.

Today the objects reach APRS-IS only, so you need an internet-connected client. This script is how a station in the area can carry them on RF. Third-party form keeps the feeder as the owner of the object and stops igates sending the packet back to the internet a second time. Within 25 miles of Norristown there are about 130 objects, which is why the script starts small. The --kiss-tcp address shown is where Direwolf listens by default; a TNC on a serial port uses --kiss-serial with its device name instead. It has keyed a real radio through Direwolf: two objects and their kills went out on 144.39. What is still unconfirmed is a second station decoding them. Bulletins for critical objects in your area are transmitted too, ahead of the objects. With --auto the script takes every object in its radius, whatever the level, and sends only two or three packets per poll from a queue: kills, then bulletins, then objects by importance, so a new fire jumps ahead of roadwork and the low ones go out only when nothing more important is waiting.
